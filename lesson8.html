Mute and Solo

Any voice can be silenced and brought back. Inside the chip, all five of those switches live in one byte, and two instructions change one switch without disturbing the rest.

In this lesson (about 25 minutes, sound on). You will be able to:
  • mute and solo voices, and hear what each one adds
  • read a byte as a row of yes-or-no switches
  • switch one bit on with OR and off with AND, leaving the others alone
You will use what lessons 1 to 7 taught: bits and binary, voices, registers, x366 instructions, and branches.

Hear it: taking voices away

Press PLAY. All five voices at once: kick, snare, hi-hat, a bass line, and the melody from lesson 3 on the lead.

In a studio, the voices of a song meet at a mixer: a desk with a row of controls for each voice, where they are balanced and combined into one sound. more

Every voice on a mixer has a button to mute it: silence it without deleting what it plays. more Press it again and the voice comes straight back.

Mute the kick and the bass together. The track suddenly floats. Producers do this on purpose before a big moment: unmute them, and the beat lands harder for having been gone.

Next to each mute is a solo button, which lets you hear one voice alone by muting all the others. more Solo the bass and listen to what it really plays. Press it again to bring everyone back.

Five buttons, one byte

REGISTER keeps all five mute buttons in one byte, at $10AA. Each voice gets one bit, from lesson 2: the kick is bit 0, on the right, then the snare, lead, bass and hi-hat. A 1 means muted. The top three bits are not used.

Press the mute buttons above again and watch the bits change. A single bit used as a yes-or-no switch is a flag. more This byte holds five flags, one per voice.

Changing one flag

Say a program should mute the lead. MOV AL, 4 then MOV [0x10AA], AL would set the lead's flag, but it writes all eight bits at once. Any other voice that was muted would come back on. The program has to change one bit and leave the other seven as they were.

A number used to pick which bits to change is a bitmask. more The lead's bitmask is 0b00100: a 1 on the lead's bit and 0 everywhere else. x366 writes binary with 0b in front, the way it writes hex with 0x.

The first tool is OR. It combines two numbers bit by bit, and each bit of the answer is 1 if either bit is 1. more

BitMask bitBit OR mask bit
000: stays as it was
101: stays as it was
011: switched on
111: already on

So OR with a bitmask switches on the mask's bits and leaves every other bit alone. Mute the kick with its button first, then run this, one line at a time:

Switching a flag off

  
The second tool is AND. It combines two numbers bit by bit, and each bit of the answer is 1 only if both bits are 1. more

  

    
BitMask bitBit AND mask bit

    
010: stays as it was

    
111: stays as it was

    
100: switched off

    
000: already off

  

  
So AND works the other way round. Its bitmask has a 0 on the bit to switch off and a 1 on every bit to keep. To unmute the lead, the mask is 0b11011.

Hear it again

  
Now the mixer makes sense from the inside. A mute button is a flag. The row of mute buttons is one byte. Pressing one button is an OR or an AND with that voice's bitmask: the one bit changes, and the rest of the mix stays as it was.

  
Solo is the two together. Solo the bass means OR with 0b11111 to mute everyone, then AND with 0b10111 to bring the bass back. Press a solo button above and watch the byte: that is what it did.

  
You have used flags since lesson 1, too. PLAY writes 3 to $1500, which is 0b11: bit 0 says play, and bit 1 says loop.

Now answer these out loud before you open them.

  
The mute byte is 0b01001. Which voices are muted?
The kick (bit 0) and the bass (bit 3). Count bits from the right, starting at 0. See "Five buttons, one byte".

  
Why not just MOV AL, 4 and store it, to mute the lead?
That writes the whole byte, so every other flag becomes 0 and every other voice comes back on. OR changes only the bits in the mask. See "Changing one flag".

  
The snare is already muted. What does OR AL, 0b00010 do to it?
Nothing: 1 OR 1 is 1. OR can only switch bits on. See the OR table.

  
Which bitmask unmutes the kick with AND?
0b11110: a 0 on the kick's bit, bit 0, and a 1 on every bit to keep. See "Switching a flag off".

The big idea: a byte can hold eight separate yes-or-no answers. OR switches chosen bits on, AND switches chosen bits off, and a bitmask says which. That is how a mixer, or any machine full of switches, keeps its settings in a few bytes.
